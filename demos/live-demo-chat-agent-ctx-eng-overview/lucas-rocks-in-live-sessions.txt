Coding an agent from scratch is undeniably the best way to learn context engineering 
because it forces you to confront every decision about what information goes into 
the context window, when it gets added, and why it matters — there are no abstractions
hiding the complexity from you. When you build an agent by hand, you must deliberately
design how the agent perceives its environment, retains memory, manages conversation
history, and passes instructions across multiple steps, making every trade-off in token
usage, relevance, and structure immediately visible and consequential. Frameworks and
pre-built tools can scaffold these decisions for you, but they also obscure the reasoning
behind them; building from scratch means you feel the pain of a bloated or poorly structured
context firsthand, and you develop an intuition for crafting prompts and state management
that no tutorial or high-level library can replicate. This hands-on struggle transforms
context engineering from an abstract concept into a deeply understood skill, because you
are not just consuming a pattern — you are inventing it, breaking it, and refining it in real time.
